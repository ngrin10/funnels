# Learn who this is for

Use real customer evidence when available. Do not make up a buyer profile and then treat it as research.

## Read and sort

Read the notes, calls, reviews, support questions, and product facts in scope. Keep who said each line, when, and what they were talking about. Tell customer words apart from sales claims and internal ideas.

| Topic | What to find | What it can change |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | What the buyer wants to get done | The main promise |
| Current problem | What is hard today, in their words | The opening and screen job |
| Past attempts | What they tried and what happened | The explanation and comparison |
| Questions and worries | What they need to know before taking the next step | Content order and proof |
| Buying context | Who decides, who pays, who uses it, and what must be ready | Routing, terms, and handoff |
| Product knowledge | What they already know about the problem and offer | How much explanation is needed |
| First value | What makes the purchase useful in practice | Delivery and onboarding |

Record direct quotes, paraphrases, and guesses as different things. A few calls do not prove what most buyers think. Keep contrary evidence and note which groups are not covered.

## Turn evidence into choices

For each proposed message or screen block, name the customer question it answers and the source behind it. If there is no source, mark it as a proposal to check.

Write a short argument: who has the problem, what the product does, why that may help, what supports it, what it cannot do, and what the buyer should do next. Do not invent a new product mechanism to make the story more exciting.

Ask about gaps that change the work. Example: "For the **main promise**, is saving time the buyer's stated goal, or our guess? This changes the opening."

## Keep research separate from voice

Customer quotes help find the message. A voice sample helps decide how it sounds. Use an approved writing sample when possible. Do not copy the broken rhythm of a call transcript into finished prose. Preserve the buyer's meaning without turning every quote into jargon.

Save the useful findings in the private context doc. Carry only needed facts and approved words into the builder pack.
