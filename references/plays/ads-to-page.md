# Ads to page

This file helps the page match the ad. It does not teach media buying. Get a paid-media skill for budgets, bidding, and account setup.

## Know how aware the visitor is

| They know | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Nothing about the problem | A story or picture they see themselves in |
| The problem, not the fix | The cause of the problem, in their words |
| Fixes exist, not yours | Why your way is different (the mechanism) |
| Your product | Proof, offer terms, and a clear next step |
| Your product and want it | The offer and the button |

Sort ads and pages by this table. A page built for one level will lose visitors at another.

## Plays to test

| Play | Why it can work | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Same words and same person on page as on ad | A mismatch feels like the wrong page | Bounce, next step rate |
| "Because of <new thing>, <group> can now <result>" hook | A real new reason makes an old promise feel fresh | Click to sale, not just click rate |
| Vary who the ad is for, not just the format | Different people buy for different reasons | Cost per buyer by group |
| Copy a winning page and change one thing | Several winners can run side by side for different buyers | Cost per buyer per version |
| Plain, fast pages | Simple pages often beat polished ones | Load time, next step rate |
| Do not ask for a phone number at first opt-in | Each field costs sign ups | Opt in rate and revenue per lead |
| Keep a hook that works and change the path | The same hook can sell through a webinar, a cheap product, or a DM | New buyers per path |

## What to count

Count cost per buyer, cost per held call, or money in. A high ad cost with strong buying is fine. Low ad cost with no buyers is not.

Spend a test budget fast enough to learn, then decide. Get a test-design skill for how much and how long.
