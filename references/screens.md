# Screen brief and design craft

Start with the real flow and the current design system. Do not design around a promise the product cannot fulfill. Read the evidence guide and the relevant surface guide first.

## Write the brief before drawing

- Moment: what happened just before this screen, and who is here.
- Job: what useful choice or task this screen supports.
- Questions: what the person needs to know, in likely order, with evidence or a proposal label.
- Main content: the object, fact, or number needed for that choice; its source and missing state.
- Worry and help: the real concern and the allowed answer, without inventing protection or a guarantee.
- Action: main button, destination, other choices, and what happens next.
- Limits: words, terms, data, or claims that must not appear.
- System: the real shell, components, tokens, type, spacing, icons, and device rules.

Use a table to link each block to a reader question. Remove a block that has no clear job. Do not make all cards, headings, and actions equally loud.

## Make the decision easy to read

Give the key content a clear place in the reading order. Let the main action stand out. Explain a confusing amount near the amount. Keep units, dates, and the basis of a comparison visible. Put alternatives where they can be found without making them compete with every other element.

Use the product's color meanings and accessible labels. Do not force a fixed color scheme from a reference. A total may need space and type weight rather than another box. When a screen feels crowded, cut or move less useful content before shrinking the text.

For a new brand, propose a visual direction with a reason tied to the audience and job. For an existing product, match its style. Do not ban common fonts, add a new theme for novelty, or force a new grid.

## Map design to real data

Use the evidence table for each visible value and action. Label sample content in mocks. Show missing, loading, error, success, disabled, long-text, and repeat states. Do not ship raw merge fields, fake results, fixed values that belong in config, or a button for a missing feature.

## Draw and check

One DEV section per change, in the spec's order:

1. Today: live image and date, or a labeled stand-in.
2. Next: revised screen using existing components.
3. Note beside the screen: what to build, event checks, main measure, status, and links.

Keep work notes outside the customer screen. Link the verified section back to the spec. If the tool lists only part of a file, check available pages before declaring a frame missing. If access fails, deliver the brief and say "not drawn".

Inspect each render. Check reading order, competing actions, text size, clipped content, long values, total math, phone layout, keyboard, focus, zoom, and reduced motion. Compare with the real product at the same size. Record which states and devices were checked; do not call an unviewed render done.
