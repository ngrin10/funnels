# Health offers

Health, weight loss, supplements, peptides, and telehealth have strict rules. Every claim, guarantee, and ad needs approval from the right qualified person. This file helps you ask better questions. It does not approve anything.

## Plays to test

| Play | When it fits | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Short path: simple ad, short page, then the medical intake | The buyer already knows the treatment and only doubts trust and cost | Intake finish rate, approval rate, paid patients |
| Teach first, then sell | The treatment is new to most people | Cost per paid patient |
| Tell a true "how it works" story, not a promise | Claims are limited | Watch time, sale rate, approvals |
| Talk to one tight group | One age or life stage gets better results and stays longer | Results, stay length, cost |
| Ongoing help as the real business | The first sale costs a lot, so profit comes from staying | Months stayed, gross profit per patient |
| "The goal of this program is X" instead of a promised result | Guarantees of health outcomes are risky | Approval from the right person |

## Hard checks before any build

- Who reviews claims, pages, ads, and intake questions? Name them.
- What data is health data here, and where may it go? Ad tracking on health pages may be limited. Get an analytics and privacy owner.
- What does a buyer get if the provider says no? Define refund and message rules.
- Model the full chain (intake, approval, ship, refill) before spending. Teams have lost a lot by skipping steps in this math.

Public talks report that cost per patient is high and payback takes months in this field. Treat that as **STATED** and model this project's own numbers with a finance skill.
