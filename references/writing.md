# Plain words and a real voice

Default to fifth-grade words in chat, specs, briefs, and handoffs. Use short, clear sentences that show how ideas connect. Keep exact code names when the builder needs them, then explain what they mean.

Use [plain terms](plain-terms.md) when a technical word is needed.

Use "people in this group" before "cohort". Say "what this number counts" before "metric definition". Say "turn it off and check the old flow" before "rollback". Never simplify by losing a rule, changing a number, or hiding a limit.

## Edit in this order

1. Check facts, terms, claims, quotes, and dates. Do not polish a claim that lacks support.
2. Use the person's approved voice sample. Note words, sentence rhythm, tone, and what they would never say. If none exists, use plain, direct prose and say that voice matching has not been checked.
3. Replace vague praise with the actual fact. "Manage invoices in one place" is clearer than "unlock a seamless financial experience" when that is what the product does.
4. Cut repeated points, canned openings, forced contrasts, slogan endings, vague sources, and decorative words.
5. Mix sentence lengths naturally. Connect related thoughts. Do not force odd grammar or ban a useful list just to seem human.
6. Read the draft aloud. Rewrite spots that sound stiff or hard to follow. Match the channel: a spec can use tables; a short text message should read like a message.
7. Check facts again. Edits must not add confidence, a testimonial, an anecdote, a deadline, or a promise.

Do not claim that the text will pass an AI detector. Do not invent a personal story or feeling to make it sound human. A grade-level score can flag hard prose; it cannot prove clarity or accuracy.

## Plain limit statements

- "I don't know which price is right. These two sources disagree."
- "I can draft the screen. This part needs a tracking skill before we can judge it."
- "This number went down. We have not shown why."
- "The data is missing. That does not mean nobody did it."

Keep internal checks out of the customer's screen. Put the facts they need in the screen and the work notes in the spec.
