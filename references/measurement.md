# Measurement and test plans

Define the main outcome before ranking a change or writing a test. A purchase selection is not a paid order. A booked call is not a held call. A renewal due is not money collected.

Every quoted number needs: source and account/project, pulled date, window, cohort/eligibility, count unit, numerator/denominator for a rate, currency or other unit, and MEASURED/STATED/MODELED label. Include the exact query or report when possible. Preserve unknowns and conflicts.

For impact estimates, use eligible people per week × expected absolute change in outcome rate × value per outcome at a stated horizon. State whether value is revenue, margin, or collected cash. Label the expected lift as a guess and the result MODELED. Show costs, effort, and confidence separately. An estimate is not proof of what caused a result.

For a test, decide the hypothesis, control and variant, randomization unit, stable assignment, allocation, exclusions, exposure event, main outcome, guardrails, outcome delay, sample plan and stopping method. Use the baseline, minimum worthwhile change, traffic, and agreed statistical method to plan the sample. If these are missing, mark the test plan incomplete. Do not invent a sample size or declare a winner after a fixed number of conversions.

Define failure stops separately from success calls. A broken checkout may require an immediate stop; ordinary metric noise does not justify an unplanned early win. Cover weekday effects, delayed payments, refunds, retention and mature cohorts where relevant. Low-traffic work may need qualitative evidence or a staged rollout, clearly labeled as having weaker causal evidence.

For a fix, document rollout and recovery without forcing an experiment or holdout. Confirm tracking in the right account before reading results. Report the observed result, uncertainty, and limits of the design.
