# Plays: patterns worth testing

A play is a pattern that has worked for other teams. It is a test idea for this project. It is not a rule, a promise, or proof.

These plays come from public talks, podcasts, and courses by people who run funnels for a living. They were rewritten here in plain words. Any number in a play is **STATED** (someone said it on the record). Nobody here checked it. Never copy a number into a spec as this project's goal.

## How to use a play

1. Finish step 2 (ground it in facts) first. A play cannot replace this project's own data.
2. Open the play file for the part of the path you are changing. Read the whole file.
3. Pick a play only if the project's facts fit it. Write why in the spec: "Play: <name>. Fits because <fact and source>."
4. Write what would prove it wrong. Name the result that counts, such as paid sales or held calls, not clicks.
5. Mark the change **PROPOSED** until the owner approves it.

If no play fits, say so. Do not force one.

## Rules that apply to every play

- Judge a test on money or real value at a named time, not on the first click. A page that gets more leads and fewer buyers lost.
- Keep one promise and one frame from the ad to the call or first use. Mixed frames hurt every later step.
- Change one main thing per test unless the owner chose a bigger rebuild on purpose.
- Fix a broken step before you add a new one.
- A play that broke a law, a platform rule, or a customer's trust is not a play. Health, money, and legal claims need the right person to approve them.
